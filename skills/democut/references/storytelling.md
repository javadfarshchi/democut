# Writing a demo people watch to the end

1. **Start with the viewer's problem.** First 10 seconds: who is stuck, and on what. The product name can wait.
2. **One persona, one story.** "Dana has 300 invoices and a deadline on Friday" beats a feature list.
3. **Three to five chapters.** Each answers one question the viewer has ("Where do I look first?", "What can wait?").
   Chapter cards give rhythm and become MP4 chapters and player-page links.
4. **One idea per shot, 6 to 12 seconds.** If a shot needs two sentences joined by "and also", split it.
5. **Say why, not what.** The screen already shows "she clicks Save". The voice says what that buys her.
6. **Write for the ear.** Short sentences, numbers spelled the way they are said, no parentheses, no acronyms the
   viewer has not heard yet. Read it aloud once.
7. **Zoom on the payoff,** not on every click. One zoom per shot at most.
8. **Cut waiting.** Never record a spinner inside a scene; navigate and load outside scenes.
9. **Be honest on screen.** Badge test data, mark shortened output, and call out a visible known bug with a `note`.
10. **Two lengths.** A full cut (2 to 3 min) for people who chose to watch, and a short cut (30 to 60 s) for social
    posts and the README. Captions are burned in because most people watch muted.
11. **End with one action:** install command, URL, or "book a call". Not three.
